Reincarnation Through Karma

The cleaned-up rules and concepts from the supplied text file.

Introduction

“Game Over. Your karma value is being calculated. Please wait a moment. While you wait, here are the rules.”

Upon death, you arrive to be judged by your karma. You may use it to decide your next life, including perks, background, physique, and special abilities.

Karma represents the good your actions accomplish, regardless of your thoughts or intentions. Your remaining good karma becomes providence, or good luck.

CommentsAbout the ideas behind this CYOA

This CYOA was inspired by a xianxia story where the main character could reincarnate based on his achievements. He could assign points to qualities such as Dao Heart, Comprehension, Talent, and Background. That idea felt like a CYOA to me.

I had been trying to write a story around that idea, but struggled to visualize it. I wanted to create my own CYOA version of it. That is how the Karma CYOA began.

Karma and a new beginning

In my opinion, karma comes from the actions a person takes, not simply from the thoughts they have. Everyone can think of themselves as a good person, but not everyone acts on those thoughts. Creating a positive impact comes first in this CYOA.

I also wanted replayability. Each lifetime is a new beginning: when you die, you return to the CYOA and fill it out again, this time carrying the karma you earned. Did you do good? Did you help a lot of people?

Why patrons, loans, and missions?

The patrons came about while chatting with Paper_tank. We were looking for a way to address a small starting budget without pretending the character had earned points they had not. Karma comes from good deeds, so I was thinking about a reasonable way for more points to enter the system. Patrons came to mind: they can loan you karma points in exchange for watching your life.

I did not want patrons to be static. I remembered the idea that leftover karma could become a form of good luck, and wondered: why not make patrons a choice about the genre of life you want to experience?

I have not covered every genre, but I developed the idea of Good, Neutral, and Bad / Evil patrons. Bad patrons give people with negative karma an actual way to play. Since that gives bad karma an advantage, Good patrons needed an advantage too: they double the points from the past, which gives decent folks an extra 20 points for free. I was not sure what to give Neutral patrons, so I gave them missions.

Loans also helped solve Paper_tank's point-budget issue. Nothing comes for free: patrons are investing in you. The more points you borrow, the more they can influence your destiny toward the kind of life they want to see. That gives the borrowed points a reason to exist.

An idea still in progress

I am still considering how to handle lives that do not have many points. One possibility is that, in the previous life before this one, you needed to repay a debt to your patron.

Core rules

  • You do not retain memories of previous lives unless you purchase a perk or physique that allows it.
  • If you die as a baby, all spent points are returned and you can try again.
  • Powers that do not fit a world's system acclimatize and become variants compatible with that world's power system.
  • When a World Type is selected, powers with an affinity for that world appear together in the Local subsection under Powers. The discount is half the affinity percentage: 100% affinity gives 50% off, 75% gives 37.5% off, 50% gives 25% off, 25% gives 12.5% off, and 0% gives no discount. Inside Local, Local Power Talent offers one world-specific aptitude tier: Minor (10), Moderate (20), High (40), or Genius (80); it does not automatically grant a power.
  • Constitution is your body's limit and training ceiling. Normally it sets the hard allocation limit for each other attribute at twice your modified Constitution; Broken Limits removes that cap.
  • Appearance Maxing is an accordion under Allocate Stats. Enter any real-world or fictional ethnicity yourself, then choose features in their own accordions. Some facial and body-type choices require allocated Constitution or Strength; randomizers are available for the body and each feature group.
  • Numeric physique modifiers apply to final stats and stack multiplicatively. Modified Constitution sets the allocation cap for other attributes at twice its value. The Broken Limits physique choice removes that cap; it allows your body to go beyond its Constitution limit, but does not make progress continue at the same pace.
  • You may choose one patron before choosing your past. A Good patron doubles karma earned from good past tiers; an Evil patron reverses the karma effect of every past tier. Neutral patrons leave past karma unchanged and offer missions for additional karma.
  • Taking a patron loan adds its selected points to your available karma. In exchange, your patron nudges your fate toward the kind of life they want to witness. Loan tiers are chosen one at a time, and Neutral-patron missions are only available for the patron you selected.
  • Some choices may require other choices or be incompatible with them. Required choices must be selected first. Incompatible choices cannot be selected together; unavailable choices remain visible but are dimmed with an explanation.
  • The point costs for most backgrounds, physiques, perks, powers, and drawbacks were not specified in the supplied source. They are set to zero in the builder until you assign them.

Stat allocation

Default allocation: 30 points. One karma point equals one stat point. The source says an average stat is 5 and 10 is a super-genius level; above 10 is beyond genius. Enter allocated points directly in each attribute's number box. Use the adjacent buy and sell controls to add or remove unspent attribute points at one karma per point. Each non-Constitution attribute is limited to twice your modified Constitution; increasing Constitution or selecting a Constitution modifier raises those limits.

  • Strength: ability to lift.
  • Endurance: ability to keep moving.
  • Agility: ability to move quickly.
  • Constitution: body's limit and training ceiling; the hard limit for allocated points is twice Constitution.
  • Perception: senses, including noticing social cues and seeing or smelling better.
  • Intelligence: referenced by physique modifiers. The source does not provide a separate definition.

Editorial note: spelling, grammar, punctuation, and consistency have been cleaned up while retaining the source's concepts. Where the source was ambiguous or incomplete, this guide flags the gap rather than inventing a rule.
